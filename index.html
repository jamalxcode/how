      What is How?

      How answers three questions about any thing — a combustion engine, scissors, a kettle, a zipper, a refrigerator, bicycle gears, how a house is built: how does it work, how is it made, and what does it cost?

      • How it works. One big exploded view with the parts numbered, a few short steps and a fun fact, plus photos of the real thing from Wikipedia and a 3D model from Sketchfab to spin.
      • How it is made. The factory, in the order things happen: raw materials, parts made or bought, machines, assembly, quality checks, packing and shipping. Every step is marked machine, people or both, with a floor plan drawn to scale and before-and-after pictures of every zone, in three versions: hand-made, mixed and automated.
      • What it costs. Approximate numbers for all three versions side by side: land, people and monthly salaries, equipment, power and running costs, and how long each takes to pay back at the markup you choose. Change any number to your own and it all recalculates.

      It explains in 17 languages, including Arabic, Urdu and Persian laid out right to left, for a curious adult, a 10-year-old or an engineering student. It began in the spirit of the old Tell Me Why books — almost no reading, one big picture — and grew into a first look at making the thing too.

      The words and plans are written by Claude and the pictures are painted by OpenAI's image model, both paid for through your own OpenRouter account (a few cents a page; a factory plan or a cost estimate is a few cents more). Every answer in the gallery, and every answer someone shares with you, opens free with no sign-in. The factory plans and costs are rough estimates for learning and a first feel of the numbers, not engineering quotes. Made by Sala Company in Kuwait; the source is open.
